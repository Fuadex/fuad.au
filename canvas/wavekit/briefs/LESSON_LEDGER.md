# LESSON LEDGER — every numbered lesson from waves 17–34 (1–100) (split from BRIEF_common 2026-11-05)

One lesson per past defect, numbered for citation. **Read whole only if you are the AUDITOR or a
SEAL role** — the ledger is their checklist. Every other agent looks up the specific numbers its
stage brief or its audit cites, and nothing more. New waves append their lessons here.


## WAVE-34 LESSONS (2026-10-29, fifteen works, audit + cold pass + two-half seal) — binding
95. **A tighter image is checked against the WHOLE object.** Two wave-34 "upgrades" were details — a Morisot cut at the
    parasol, an Ōkyo screen missing a cub. A crop, a tile or a larger file is never the plate until its edges and its
    figures have been counted against the plate's.
96. **A verification aid may check, never describe.** Gold dust visible only in a larger reference image, not on the plate
    the reader zooms, was cut from an Ōkyo stop. Reader text describes what the plate shows.
97. **No gift, sale or purchase line goes to the Info**, whatever a fusion allocates — the fusion did it twice in wave 34 (a
    1991 gift, a State purchase). A purchase may reach the Interpretation only as reception: what the buying said about
    the picture.
98. **A body part is named at the plate by measurement.** Wave 34's "heel" was a knee (about two and a half times the
    heel's size, with a thigh-to-calf fold). A part a source names is matched at the plate before the text names it.
99. **A holder's reading of the visible is a plate claim.** A museum text's "the perspective lines seem to diverge" failed
    at the plate. A curator's description of what the picture shows is checked like any eye claim.
100. **An undated work stays undated.** A range's midpoint shown as a year is not a date; write the range or "undated",
    never a year the record does not give.

## WAVE-33 LESSONS (2026-10-22, five works, audit + cold pass + three-role seal) — binding
91. **The form of a source is a fact.** Wave 33 called Sisley's own LETTER to Tavernier "the writer's report", and a
    preface to a dealer's album "a review". Who wrote it, to whom, and in what form is checked like a date.
92. **Gaze and grip verbs are plate claims.** "Turns from" (she looks past), "flung out" (he holds it out): a verb that says
    where a face points or what a hand does is checked at the plate at 4x, like a count.
93. **A repetition claim is counted.** "The skin is shown twice" rested on one ring placed by geometry. Before writing that a
    form repeats, name each instance and box it.
94. **A hedge in the research survives into the Info word for word in meaning.** "The place name came later" hardened the
    source's "the title is today's". Re-read the hook's DOUBTS before writing any Info clause about a title, date or name.

## WAVE-32 LESSONS (2026-10-22, three works, audit + cold pass + three-role seal) — binding
87. **A secondary's paraphrase is not its source.** Wave 32's research carried a fashion-history page's paraphrase of a dress
    historian ("full sleeves and a modest neckline") into the Interpretation; the source and the plate both said ample
    décolletage. A garment or visual claim taken from reading is also checked at the plate, and a quoted judgement at the
    page that holds it.
88. **The two-version pin binds the store's own texts too.** The store had told Leroy's 1874 joke as the reception of a
    canvas that most likely never hung. A reception story attaches only to the object exhibited; when a version question is
    open, the sentence says which object, or stays with the motif.
89. **A light claim survives the tour's own measurements on both sides.** Wave 32's "the field is not what lights her: the
    cheek turned to it is in shadow" was refuted by its own stop 2. Before writing what lights or does not light a form,
    diff it against every other brightness claim in the tour.
90. **A superlative over the plate is measured over the WHOLE plate.** "Below the sky the brightest stretch" was refuted by
    a pavement at the other end of the canvas. Scope it (the upper half, this side) or measure everything.

## WAVE-31 LESSONS (2026-10-22, five works, audit + cold pass + three-role seal) — binding
82. **An Interpretation ends on the object, never on a quoted authority.** Two wave-31 Interpretations closed on a critic's
    or curator's line with no route back to the marble or the canvas (the cold reader's repeated "dangling authority"
    MID). Keep the voice, but let the last sentence stand on something the reader can see.
83. **Name only what is identified.** An unidentified setting is "water", not "sea"; a shape read as a boat is "likely" a
    boat in every layer it reaches. A reading named as a sighting is the oldest defect class in this store.
84. **A shared observation may stay; a lesson the store already teaches is re-aimed.** Wave 31's Turner shared five lessons
    with the shipped Norham tour; each was re-aimed at what only that canvas shows (the crag's firm edges face the glow —
    measured), or cut. Check every lesson against the store's own tours of the motif before drafting.
85. **Verbs of return carry history.** A canvas painted in Paris "came to" Spain in 1981; it never "returned". Check every
    returned / restored / came back / again against where the object had actually been.
86. **The batch does not share a closing move.** Three wave-31 Interpretations closed on a located detail and two on a
    quoted authority. Read the finished Interpretations in the workshop and make your close differ in kind.

## WAVE-30 LESSONS (2026-10-21, five works, audit + cold pass + three-role seal) — binding
77. **A fusion's allocation is the orchestrator's guess.** Wave 30's drafters caught about twenty-five fusion errors — a craft
    method that was a stop's finding, an unsourced gloss ("large for a still life", "before the Flood"), a wrong venue (the
    British Institution, not the Academy). Check every allocated fact against the hook and say so out loud.
78. **A reserved claim never leaks into the `beside`.** The seal proposed stating a beside's reversal with the Interpretation's
    reserved letters; it was refused. State a reversal from the canvases themselves.
79. **A replaced read's refs die with its prose.** A legacy Info's link survived a REPLACE and failed the refs gate.
80. **Glossed or not at all.** A quoted marginal note or idiom ("Off again, G."; "sacks from the same grinding") left bare
    lost the cold reader; give its sense in a few words or leave it out.
81. **A store tour of a sister canvas owns its lessons.** Wave 30's Seine sunrise could not re-teach the shipped Seine at
    Giverny's "no drawn waterline"; re-aim at what only this canvas has.

## WAVE-29 LESSONS (2026-10-20, four works, audit + cold pass + three-role seal) — binding
73. **A sister canvas is never placed or owned in reader text.** The wave-29 seal cut a twin version's city and holder:
    a second canvas of the same view is "a second canvas of the same view", nothing more.
74. **A researcher's gloss is not the source's line.** "Afternoon sun" was the researcher's word; the curator wrote
    "an identical view … but on a rainy day". Quote or paraphrase the source, never the hook's summary of it.
75. **Narration belongs to its writer.** A critic's own scene-setting ("streets opening onto the sea") was nearly given to
    the painter. Attribute a description to the person who wrote it.
76. **A measured claim can refute its auditor.** A repair agent measured the plate and overturned an audit's proof; the
    plate decides, not the seniority of the stage.

## WAVE-28 LESSONS (2026-10-20, twenty-five works, audit + cold pass + five-role seal) — binding
66. **A fusion line is a claim to check, not a source** (lesson 46 re-proved): the wave-28 drafters caught about forty
    fusion errors — an invitation nobody issued, a letter that named no one, a "for decades" that was two catalogues, a
    merged quotation. Check every fusion fact against the hook before using it, and report the error.
67. **A medium's general process is told ONCE per batch.** Seven print crafts each re-taught the woodblock process until
    one tour was given it; the rest had to cut to what is particular to their own sheet.
68. **A closing stop boxed to the object (not the plate) under 0.90 of the plate counts as DESCENT** in coverage.js —
    box a closer past 0.90 (a sliver of wall or mount is acceptable) or it will break the guard.
69. **Batch tics the cold readers caught**: "the eye cannot settle" ended nine entries; nearly every Interpretation took
    one shape (opening paradox → named writer + year + quote → back to one located detail). Vary the opening move and
    the close; do not end a lens on the eye's indecision.
70. **A matcher's pick is not an identity.** A seen row pointed at a sister canvas because an image matcher chose it; a
    relief row pointed at a cast lost since 1964. Before drafting, settle WHICH object Fuad stood in front of.
71. **One photograph of a plaster is not a marble.** A tour drafted on the wrong cast or material describes another
    object; material decides vocabulary (bronze / plaster / marble / lead), and the plate must show the object seen.
72. **A lens may run short** (38–60 words) when the stops own the rest — never pad; but a cut that leaves a lens under
    60 or a stop under 80 is re-derived, not forced (lesson 65).

## WAVE-27 LESSONS (2026-10-20, one work, audit + cold pass + seal) — binding
62. **A derived spatial claim is geometry, not sight.** "The wall faces east, toward the morning" was inferred from a
    catalogue's light note plus the tour's "light from our left" — the plate shows neither compass. Keep spatial claims in
    the picture's own terms (our left, the sea-facing wall) unless a source states the orientation.
63. **A reserved document must pay off on the canvas.** The cold reader flagged an Interpretation that added a letter and
    took it back in the next sentence ("which canvases he meant is not recorded, and this one is…"). Tell the document only
    for what it shows about the picture, and turn it onto the canvas.
64. **An unread mark is not a date.** A blind pass read a brushmark after the signature as "the date"; two records say the
    canvas is only signed. Read signatures at 4×, and write "dated" only when research confirms the date is inscribed.
65. **A cut that breaks a band is reverted, not patched** — a stop trimmed for a LOW fell to 78 words and an 8-word closer.

## WAVE-26 LESSONS (2026-10-19/20, twenty works, audit + cold pass + three-role seal) — binding
55. **Closing stops that step back and sort the canvas into zones or bands** were the wave's biggest tic (ten of twenty).
    A closer earns its place with something the descent did not give, or the tour ends on its last real mark.
56. **"A curator" / "one scholar" / "one reading"** attributions piled up in most entries. Where the source names the
    person, name them once in the layer that carries the claim; where it is common ground, state it plainly.
57. **Payoff shapes the cold readers caught repeating**: light-direction closers; "reads as X, not Y"; "X is what does Y";
    "drawn, not modelled"; "this is what makes it read as water"; stop openers "Seen whole,", "Step back", "Close to,";
    "about a third of the way down". Write none of them by default.
58. **Interpretations opening on a quoted letter** were three of twenty. Open on the claim in the work's own terms.
59. **A term introduced only in a stop and used cold in the Interpretation loses the reader** ("the column", "the pine").
    Every read stands alone: introduce a referent in the sentence that turns on it.
60. **A cut can leave a lens skeletal** (a craft lens fell to 31 words after a cold ruling). After any cut, check that what
    remains still teaches; a short lens is fine, an empty one is not.
61. **A pale field the eye cannot settle (sky or far sea) is named as unsettled once**, in `see`, and nothing later argues
    from one reading of it.


## WAVE-25 LESSONS (2026-10-19, twenty works, audit + cold pass + three-role seal) — binding
45. **State the numbering.** Fusions and audits mixed 0- and 1-based stop numbers; every reference to a stop says which
    base it uses, and a drafter who finds the numbers off says so before writing.
46. **An allocation is not a source.** Drafters caught twelve fusion errors (ages, dates, overreaching attributions, a
    craft method that was really a stop's finding). Check every allocated fact against the hook and every allocated
    method against the stops; say so out loud.
47. **"Only", "most", "every" in a last sentence were false again** (the palest patch on the whole river; the only warm
    note; heads that turn down). Measure or scope — never close a stop on an unmeasured absolute.
48. **Dissolution bias, again**: "edges dissolve", "melting", "shimmering into" were false in four tours. Describe what the
    paint does at the plate; a firm edge is called firm.
49. **The holder is never named, not even in an event story** — "reached Warsaw", "the Sumida museum's curator", "the
    National Gallery's own description" were all cut at the seal. Attribute to "a curator" / "one reading".
50. **A research hedge survives verbatim into every layer** ("probably", "for such dances", "seems"): a hedge dropped
    between hook and Info is a seal row.
51. **Never reverse a quoted verdict.** A critic's praise ("he shows us an old view from a side we had not noticed") was
    paraphrased as faint praise ("he only showed"). Quote or paraphrase in the source's own direction.
52. **One home per fact across Info and context**: an Info fact restated in the context (with eight shared four-word runs)
    was cut at the seal. The Info owns its hook; the context tells a different moment.
53. **Batch tics the cold readers found in most entries — do not write them**: `see` ending on the eye's path ("the eye
    travels / finds first"); `about` opening "The subject is / The picture is about"; `about` closing on a summary pair
    ("X and Y go on side by side"); every `beside` counting the year gap and closing on the same return-to-home contrast;
    "Read downward / from left to right" as a stop hinge; stops closing on "the same X as Y" echoes.
54. **Undefined terms in an Interpretation lose the reader**: a label, a critic's category, a forgery test, "the
    captain's defenders" — set up each term before you turn on it.

## WAVE-24 LESSONS (2026-10-18, ten works, audit + cold pass + three-role seal) — binding
37. **Never pad to pass a gate.** A wave-24 drafter added a clause to keep a stop spread at 50; it was cut. If a band or
    spread fails, find genuine slack in the SHORTEST stop or leave the failure for the orchestrator — never lengthen.
38. **Superlatives in a stop's first or last sentence were false four times** ("the quietest passage", "the darkest
    small marks", "the only…"). Scope a comparison to what you measured, or drop it.
39. **Whose left?** Two figures' arms were placed on the wrong side. Write "our left" for the picture's left and "his
    / her left" only for a body part, and check which way the figure faces first.
40. **Two photographs disagree → the words must be true of both.** "Blue sky" became "greys and blues"; a "cream"
    became the true colour. Where only one photograph exists, colour claims that carry an argument are flagged.
41. **A banned word's synonym is the same word.** "Term" for a herm; "a handful" for a count. Avoid the move, not the
    spelling.
42. **An old tour's description of a COMPANION can be wrong** (three wave-24 companions' mv-1 tours misdescribed their
    own plates). A `beside` rests on the companion's shipped Info, its record, or a verified source — not on its tour.
43. **A reserved claim is told, then turned back onto the canvas.** The cold reader called five Interpretations
    "research notes": the reserved story stays, its apparatus (dates, document names) is compressed, and the last
    third points the eye at a named place on the picture.
44. **A date conflict gets one plain sentence, once, IN THE CONTEXT** ("He dated it 1901 beside his signature; it has
    also been dated to 1900") — never the holder named as the source, never "usually", never repeated in a second
    layer, and NEVER in the Info (RE-HOMED 2026-10-22, the Info-drift rulings: the shipped misreading made the Info
    "the dispute's one home" and produced dating-dispute doorways).

## WAVE-23 LESSONS (2026-10-15/16, the cold pass and the three-role seal) — binding
30. **One home per finding, enforced.** Wave 23's worst cold flag: one picture's backlighting was told SEVEN times
    across lenses, stops, Info, Interpretation and `beside`. Say a finding once, where it lives; elsewhere build on it.
31. **The closer adds, it does not re-summarise.** Nine of ten wave-23 tours closed on a "step back" stop that retold
    the descent. A closing stop earns its place with something the descent did not give (the picture's afterlife, a
    relation only the whole frame shows, a fact that reframes it) — or the tour closes on its last real mark.
32. **Interpretations must not share an opening move.** All ten wave-23 Interpretations opened on an "X is not Y"
    aphorism followed by a quoted remark. Open on the claim itself, in the work's own terms.
33. **`context` tells the moment as a story, not a chronology.** Year-by-year chronicles and letter digests were flagged
    as catalogue cards. Choose the one or two events that change how THIS picture reads and tell them.
34. **The batch shares no thesis.** Four wave-23 works independently argued "where the picture puts the viewer".
    Two Infos opened "In [month] [year] the journalist…". Assume a sister tour has your idea; find what only yours has.
35. **The `beside` does not restate the `about`.** In four wave-23 works the beside's relation was the about lens's
    thesis again. And no "painted N years earlier/later" formula.
36. **Quantities from the fusion are checked at the source.** Wave 23's fusion briefs carried three errors the drafters
    caught (a craft method a stop already spent; "signed" for a studio stamp; a height comparison applied to the
    wrong thing). A brief is not a source: check it against the stops and the hook, and say so out loud when it is wrong.

## WAVE-22 AND PLATE-LANE LESSONS (2026-10-08 → 10-15) — binding
22. **One photograph is not a colour authority.** Visitor photographs run warm or bright; the plate-check lane this
    week reversed "gold" to copper, "maroon" to brown, "warm" to neutral once a second image or the holder's own
    words were checked. A colour claim that matters rests on two sources or is worded so either would be true.
23. **Count, and place left/right, at the plate.** This week's corrections included a picture with three horses
    described as one, three boats called two, a dancer's raised knee called a stoop, a figure facing us called
    seen from behind. Name the count and the side only after looking.
24. **Light direction is a finding.** "Lit from the right" in a sitter's terms is OUR left. Say which you mean.
25. **The dissolution bias runs both ways.** Loose passages get called looser than they are (most often), and hard
    contours get missed on knife-painted or pointillist surfaces. Check whether a passage is loose before saying so.
26. **A dark picture is described as dark.** Lift the exposure in your own crops to read the shadows, but describe
    what the plate shows at its own brightness; a claim made only on a lifted crop says so in FLAGS.
27. **A disputed fact is attributed, never flattened** (dog vs rabbit cord; 1893 vs 1896): name both or neither.
28. **No apparatus in reader text** — "on this reproduction", "the plate", "the scan", "a second photograph" are the
    workshop talking. Say what the paint does.
29. **A last sentence that only places a signature ("Signed lower left.") is not a closer** — fold it into a clause.

## WAVE-21 LESSONS — binding
18. **A viewpoint is a finding, checked at the plate.** Wave 21's fusion read a tower "climbed from below" from the blind
    pass's wording; the audit found the whole canvas seen from the air. Where the eye and the research disagree about
    where the viewer stands, settle it at the plate (what is seen from above / below) before any layer argues it.
19. **Identity before facts.** A plate's file name carried a sister canvas's size; a plate row pointed at a different
    painting altogether (Renoir, Peaches). Confirm the plate shows THIS object (proportions, details the record names).
20. **"Opposite" must be opposite.** A beside that calls two pictures' moves opposite must name two things on one scale.
21. **A craft that names no object is fine short.** If every method is a stop's finding, write less — never preview.

## WAVE-20 LESSONS — binding (each one cost a repair, a cold flag or a seal row last wave)
11. **"First", "earliest", "only", "never" are claims, not emphasis.** Wave 20 called Zola the earliest viewer of a
    picture two April reviews had already described. Prove an absolute in the source or drop it.
12. **The Info hook is never a sale or an owner.** A sale survives only as a reception event (what the market said
    about the picture), and only if it is the best hook. No collector, dealer, museum purchase or gift.
13. **Hedges travel intact.** "Probably", "one account identifies", "is reported to have said" reach the reader as
    written; "meant for" never hardens into "ordered for". Attribute lightly — never "as a 2014 article reports" (a
    researcher's note) — but keep the attribution.
14. **No researcher's or photographer's voice in reader text.** "The photographer's light", "neither yet read",
    "the crests catch the flash": say what the PAINT does.
15. **`craft` names the method; it must not list the stops' finds.** Wave 20's Signac craft named the rope, spar,
    stone joint and grass blade the stops then found, and the cold reader felt every stop as a confirmation. Say how
    the picture was made (process, materials, sequence, tools), not which objects show it.
16. **A stop's first sentence places the eye** ("At lower right, …", "Right of centre, …") before it argues.
17. **Companions are checked at their plates.** The Osbert tour had shipped a halo "dissolving into the sky" that sits
    on a dark hill. A `beside` describes what the companion's plate shows, not what its old tour says.

## WAVE-17 LESSONS — binding
1. **An eye claim is not a fact until a second look agrees.** If a claim rests on one photograph, one zoom level or
   one reading, say so where you make it.
2. **Theses are findings, not allocations.** State what is on the canvas and what it amounts to; never "the painter
   gives / spends / withholds".
3. **`craft` teaches methods no stop teaches.** The method behind several stops, never one stop's instance.
4. **No ratio from pixel values in prose.** A plain comparative ("far darker than").
5. **The Info's hook is the making, the artist's life, the technique or the reception — never the ownership.**
6. **No personal identifier in any web request.** User-Agent carries `https://fuad.au`, never an email.

## WAVE-18 LESSONS — binding (each one cost a seal round last wave)
7. **Stock lessons are checked against the store before they are taught.** Wave 18's seal cut lessons already in
   dozens of tours: complementary/simultaneous contrast (48 works), shade built from blue (6), "lit from inside"
   (39), "resolve into" (162), "hinge" (147), "stops being" (83), "does two jobs" (29), "the canvas weave shows" (61),
   "a handful of strokes", "aerial perspective". A lesson belongs in a tour only if THIS picture earns it.
8. **A visual claim made only in an Interpretation or a `beside` gets its own second look** — it has not been
   through crop QC. Keep the reads' visual claims to what the tour already verified, or flag the new one.
9. **No closing epigrams** in any layer — a last sentence that turns into an aphorism ("has not yet asked", "the
   paint has her hang") is booked at the seal.
10. **A companion's facts are facts**: its date, its CURRENT holder (on deposit? on loan?), its dimensions and what
   it shows are verified like the work's own.
