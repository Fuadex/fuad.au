# Song-read stress tests — the difficult-case ledger

The flagship "What's it about?" reads (the fable tier — see ALBUM_READS.md) run on a
deliberately frozen methodology: a minimal drafting brief, one solo writer per song, a
mechanical originality gate (max ONE shared content-word pair with the lyric, no quote
marks), redo-with-replacement-specificity, then a human-side QC pass with independent
web verification. This file is the proof ledger: the cases chosen or encountered
BECAUSE they should break the system, and what actually happened. Every claim here is
reproducible from the shipped reads.

## The limit model (established 2026-08-10, still standing)

Failure risk is not complexity — dense songs are fuel, not danger. The pressure variable
is **citation pressure**: how much of the lyric's distinct language a read must engage
versus how much exists. Sparse + famous = gate squeeze. At the ceiling the system does
not produce wrong reads — it produces escalating redo cost and then honest retreat
through existing contracts (the resists-reading counter, citation-of-form rulings,
refusal, the footnote layer).

## The ledger

### Porcupine Tree — the pressure-cooker pair (2026-08-10)

**Fear of a Blank Planet** set the worst first-gate failure of its era: 15 of 16 drafts
failed the originality gate (the famous-English-album class at full strength). Twelve
were fixed in one redo; **Anesthetize needed four rounds**, and produced the ledger's
most important mechanism-finding: *redo loops drain the image pool they need.* Each
failed draft reaches for the nearest remaining lyric image, which then gets banned; on a
sparse section three rounds consumed most of the scene's licensed vocabulary, and the
survivor could only describe the movement one abstraction level up. Diagnostic adopted:
when a read's evidence is mostly section-function plus external fact, the methodology is
at its ceiling for that song. A tested variant (feeding the exhausted image-families up
front with an invent-don't-reach instruction) recovered the sacrificed imagery but lost
the shipped read's best abstract discoveries — a different trade, not a better read;
both versions ship side-by-side so the trade stays inspectable. FoaBP also hosted the
**album-read emergence experiment**: asking the synthesizer what the track reads
*together reveal* instead of their shared throughline produced a clearly stronger liner
at the cost of one essay tic — both versions ship on the album entry, still awaiting a
ruling.

### Meshuggah — Bleed (Class D: meaning outside the text, 2026-08-10)

The inverse limit: the words are deliberately mechanical and the meaning lives in the
performance. First draft reproduced the lyric's machine coinages; the fix was the
knowledge lens carrying what text can't — the drum-endurance fact became the argument
(the words' machinery and the music's machinery as one engine). Approved as drafted.

### TesseracT — One (Class A: the multi-part suite, 2026-08-11)

First full suite test: each movement treated as a song (own key, own lyric, standard
brief), with the suite-level connective tissue riding verified footnotes instead of the
reads. Verdict: the frozen pipeline handles suites as-is. Two new error subclasses were
caught and countered: suite-position confabulation (movements claiming to be closers)
and redo-imported quotation (a register-only redo importing a lyric line the original
never had).

### Coaltar of the Deepers — No Thank You: the flatness batch + full-album fresh-draw A/B (2026-08-11→12)

The owner flagged a batch as flat, which produced the **flatness mechanism**: a read
that doesn't KNOW the song after analysis resorts to cheaply following the lyric — thin
text plus no knowledge-lens claim equals following, not knowing. The repair (fresh
draws, same frozen brief, judged blind) beat flattened redo-survivors 3 of 3 and strong
incumbents 2 of 6, while two fresh draws failed gates the originals had passed — so
fresh draws remain a repair for flagged batches, never a standing mechanism.

### Nevermore — the whole-catalog worst case (2026-08-27)

41 of 52 first-gate failures across a single catalog campaign — the record at album
scale — absorbed through standard redo waves plus twelve hand-seals, with the
verification layer sealing a chain of received-lore claims down to what sources
actually support.

### Dream Theater — Six Degrees of Inner Turbulence (the commissioned stress test, 2026-08-28)

Chosen deliberately as the standing unhandled limit: a 42-minute suite scrobbled as
movement-tracks, several without library keys, sharing an album with five standalone
songs. Results:

- **12 of 12 first-gate failures — the worst first gate ever recorded** — absorbed in a
  SINGLE redo wave, no third rounds. The sparse clinical vocabulary of the suite's
  case-portraits (each movement portrays a different psychological condition) is
  maximum citation pressure, and the redo law produced some of the campaign's best
  invented language under it.
- **The Class-A suite ruling generalizes**: movements as songs, no suite context in any
  brief, tissue in verified footnotes. No drafter needed the suite to read its movement;
  the six-strangers design and the finale's stigma argument landed as footnotes.
- **Zero position-claim errors** — the class that plagued earlier suites has been fully
  retired by a standing brief constraint.
- **One recurring error class confirmed**: a female-POV portrait voiced male by the
  drafter (the second such catch). A voice/POV question now rides the QC pass
  permanently: whose voice is this — gender, persona, narrator versus subject.

## What the ledger licenses

The methodology's core has survived: the worst gate rates on record, both limit
directions (meaning-in-performance and maximum citation pressure), suite structures,
whole-catalog scale, and adversarial selection. Its failure modes are enumerable and
each carries a standing counter. Changes since freezing have been QC-side questions
only — the drafting brief itself has not needed an edit since 2026-07-27.
