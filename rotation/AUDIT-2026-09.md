# Rotation — September 2026 audit (synthesis)

Three recon lanes (product walk · competitive landscape · backend/data architecture), run
2026-09-21 against a stable tree after the September program closed, synthesized here with
judgments and a ranked plan. Lane reports live in the session workshop; every figure in this
document was measured or repo-verified, not estimated.

## Verdict, up front

Rotation's core is unmatched: no product in the field joins a 20-year history to a lyric-level
editorial layer, and several of its data moats (measured audio DNA, per-member lineups,
city-grade geography, pre-streaming coverage) literally cannot be rebuilt by a competitor
today. Its sins are not content sins — the product walk's kindest and most useful line was
*"nothing in the top 15 needs new content."* The sins are **repetition** (Overview reprints
Stories), **burial** (the best pages and the essay are the hardest to reach), and a **payload
diet** it hasn't taken (3.73 MB gz baseline on every route, with ~1.9 MB of it unread by most
pages). The backend is structurally healthy — the monolith earns its keep — and the one live
bug the audit found was of a class killed at its source the same day (2b7c0e5).

## What works (verified, not flattered)

- **The editorial layer.** 28,752 lyric-mood rows + 28,375 theme rows joined to the history;
  4,802 hand-QC'd close reads; an authored portrait closing a nine-chapter argument. Nothing
  else in the field has *any* of these at library scale.
- **Data hygiene as a feature.** The fold ledger (694 groups), the pins correction layer (126
  entries, 93 mbids, pin-first across every consumer), songs-anchored identity verification.
  Competitors show whatever last.fm hands them; Rotation argues with its sources and wins.
- **Irreplaceable stores.** ~36,900 tracks of Spotify audio features (API killed Nov 2024,
  loopholes closed Feb 2026); per-member lineups for 1,061 bands with tenure/roles/gender;
  MB city-grade origins (82 countries / 939 cities); scrobbles back to 2006.
- **The leaf pages.** Artist/album/track pages are dense, honest, and personal; the walk
  called the writing "genuinely excellent" throughout.
- **The negative space.** Shelves models 6,565 unplayed LPs; Blind spots reasons from the
  library's own gravity. Nobody else models what you *haven't* heard.
- **Zero third-party runtime dependencies, offline-capable PWA, nightly self-rebuild.**

## What doesn't (adjudicated)

The product lane's top-15, weighed against owner intent where they collide:

1. **Overview is a teaser deck that prints the whole story.** Six cards have one-to-one
   Stories counterparts with identical rows; three surfaces draw the same world map. The fix
   is not deletion but a sharpened split: **Overview owns NOW** (live total, pulse, this
   week, the strip), **Stories owns MEANING** (lifetime), and the duplicated cards become
   genuine one-line teasers.
2. **The site never introduces itself.** First fold is chrome; the biggest number is 9px mono
   in a corner. One sentence and one headline-size number fix it.
3. **`#spotify` is unreachable** — zero inbound links to what the walk called the most
   original page in the product. Rename the Liked tab to Spotify, land on "What held my
   attention", make Liked the sub-view (the code already implies this shape).
4. **Stories' TOC ignores its own nine chapters** — a flat 37-crumb rail, 4,336px wide on
   mobile. Make the TOC the chapters.
5. **Ch. III runs four consecutive "one window ate everything" cards.** Owner verdicts now
   in hand: *Their era* merges into *Obsessions* (same measure, month vs week, contrast never
   stated); *Flameouts / One-day wonders* both stay but the song-vs-artist units must reach
   the labels; *Right now*'s closing sub is cut (it spends two later cards' material
   thousands of pixels early).
6. **Mouse-only flagship charts** (Explore scatter, both maps, Time hover) with clipped help
   text on mobile — the one product-wide accessibility debt.
7. **Records at 360 is a 10,880px horizontal scroller per genre** — needs a position
   indicator or pagination at minimum.
8. **Adjudicated in the owner's favor:** The Reading stays at the feed's floor — the walk
   wanted it promoted, but evidence→verdict is the feed's architecture; the chapter-TOC fix
   gives "The verdict" a visible anchor instead. The diet's 18×17 stroke chart is dense by
   design (hover/pin is the reading mode), but a "top-8 focus" default with all-18 as the
   expanded state is worth considering. The mount count-up ramp stays (owner-chosen), noted
   as a screenshot-truth cost.

## The field (2026)

- **Scale is no longer the story.** Spotify shipped "Spotify 20" (May 2026, lifetime recap);
  Wrapped went multiplayer. "20 years of listening" opens no doors now — **the depth does.**
- **The incumbent may wake up**: last.fm went independent (May 2026) promising "listening
  insights." Watch item, not threat yet — their Pro reports reach only to Dec 2016.
- **stats.fm / Finale / Volt-class**: presentation-strong, analysis-shallow, Spotify-era only.
  **ListenBrainz**: closest in spirit (year-in-music, fresh releases), no editorial layer.
- **Ten ideas worth taking** (ranked for Rotation's direction — derived insights,
  mobile-first, no social): minutes as a co-equal unit everywhere · permanent `#year/YYYY`
  recaps ("a Wrapped that never expires", all 20 browsable) · range *deep-linking* (Time’s existing custom range is not shareable via hash — the one
  honest gap; the range feature itself already exists) · a raw searchable history table + CSV export (covers the ~9.7% outside
  Explore) · the normalized fingerprint metrics (consistency/discovery/variance/
  concentration/replay per slice vs lifetime) · one rigidly-designed exportable card ·
  rank-over-time race charts · an unheard-release radar off cached MB release-groups · a
  recall deck (Canvas precedent) · daylist-style slice naming off the theme layer (last —
  gimmick-adjacent, but only Rotation could do it honestly).
- **Rejected as off-brand**: friends/leaderboards/Clubs, roast cards, collages, recommenders,
  live-scrobble social.

## Backend (measured)

- **Payload**: 32.05 MB gz total; **3.73 MB gz baseline on every route** before any view
  shard. The diet, all measured: move `ARTIST_X` out of `music-rest` (−879 KB gz every
  route); stop deploying `llm-about.js` (−5.94 MB gz per artifact — unreachable in
  production); cap `about/index.js` postings at 50 (−1.66 MB gz, −57% on that tab); defer
  the Overview map band's two mount fetches (−2.73 MB gz off the landing scroll). Together:
  baseline 3.73 → 2.85, landing-page scroll cost roughly halved, artifact −18.5%.
- **Build**: 30.9 s warm (not the documented ~11), 36 full passes over 323k rows, peak RSS
  1.27 GB. Two three-line fixes (memoize `slug()`, hoist day-key formatting) recover ~4.5 s.
  `llm-about.js` is vm-evaluated twice per build.
- **Joins**: the alias-less raw joins are **landmines, not fires** — a full rebuild of all
  412 artist folds against every store found exactly **2 artists** losing anything today
  (Scars on Broadway's Sound DNA). Fix as insurance, minutes of work.
- **The recOf bare-row class is dead at source** as of 2b7c0e5 (period rows now carry their
  full record). It had fired four times; the audit found the fourth live.
- **Tooling**: `check-jsx` is not in CI (one-step add — Babel is already installed there);
  ~55 hand-rolled shard injections deserve one `ensureShard()` with fail-open `onerror`
  (8 views currently hang forever on a 404); 4 generated files are tracked only by gitignore
  omission; a tracked route-runner probe over the 13 routes would have caught both of this
  month's outage classes.
- **Argued against, and endorsed**: do NOT modularize build-data (the monolith suits a
  grep-driven workforce), do NOT re-key the stores (a 2-row problem, 55 MB of churn), do NOT
  shard media-index, no browser in CI.

## The plan — ranked, sized

**Wave A — quick wins (all S, an afternoon each):**
A1 `llm-about.js` off the deploy list · A2 postings cap · A3 defer the two Overview mount
fetches · A4 `check-jsx` into CI · A5 gitignore the four generated files · A6 the alias-less
join insurance · A7 first-fold identity line on Overview · A8 TOC = nine chapters · A9 cut
Right now's closing sub · A10 merge Their era into Obsessions; unit labels on Flameouts/
One-day wonders · A11 Spotify tab cut (Liked → sub-view) · A12 STRUCK, corrected by the owner: custom date
ranges already exist as the Time tab’s flagship (the barcode scrubber’s clip range) — the
competitive lane proposed a feature the product ships. The honest residue, if wanted at all:
the range is component state only (not hash-serialised, so not shareable) and does not
propagate into the cross-page filter machinery the way calendar picks do.

**Wave B — the substantial ones (M):**
B1 `ARTIST_X` → lazy `artist-x.js` · B2 `ensureShard()` consolidation · B3 build perf pair ·
B4 Overview NOW/MEANING split (the teaser-ization) · B5 minutes as a co-equal unit ·
B6 permanent `#year/YYYY` recaps · B7 raw history table + CSV · B8 fingerprint metrics ·
B9 Records mobile shelf navigation · B10 touch affordances for the flagship charts.

**Wave C — design-led, owner-taste-gated (M–L):**
C1 export card · C2 rank-race charts · C3 unheard-release radar · C4 recall deck · C5 diet
top-8 focus mode · C6 slice naming · C7 a surface for the F28 reasoned-themes layer (the
deep-reads vocabulary the filter carries but no module shows).

## Candid close

The September program left the product in the best state it has ever been in — the data is
cleaner than any commercial service's, the editorial layer is unique in the field, and the
one live bug the audit surfaced belonged to a class that is now structurally extinct. What
stands between this and *great* is not new content but discipline already practiced
elsewhere in the estate: say what the site is, stop printing the same fact twice, surface
the buried best, and take the payload diet. The field's movement (Spotify 20, last.fm's
pivot) confirms the strategy rather than threatening it: everyone can do scale now; nobody
else can do depth.
